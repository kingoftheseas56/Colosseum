The top bar stays fixed with the five world pills Tankoban, Biblio, Theatre, Universes and Feria; Theatre is gold, and there is no Vinyl pill. The bottom-left two-button Colosseum/Vault dock stays fixed. All artwork, glyph assets and Fraunces font references are relative local files. The CSS and JavaScript are inline; no external scripts, fetches, or image URLs are used.

The scrolling board contains a featured hero with four manually clickable slides, a staged Next Up row, a staged Continue Watching row, and the Theatre tab bar. Discover offers Movie/Series/Anime lenses, source-logo index, a genre filter and a poster grid. Movies, Shows and Anime each switch to their complete shelf catalogues, followed by a genre mosaic. Library is also an in-place tab. The compact glass tab bar docks below the fixed top bar when its in-flow version leaves the visible scroll area. The catalogue row index starts collapsed and expands from 52px to 240px using its chevron. Index rows scroll to their own shelf and the current row is highlighted. Hover, click and basic keyboard focus affordances are present. A lightweight toast acknowledges preview-only destinations; nothing attempts to sign in or fetch data.

## Visual differences, unavoidable mismatches and guesses

The major unavoidable difference is the backdrop. Today's screenshots show a colourful sunset cityscape with arched architecture, but the explicitly required `../assets/cold-ripple.jpg` is a monochrome wooden pier in fog. I used the required asset and did not crop, copy or re-use any screenshot pixels. Because the wallpaper provides much of the scene's colour and brightness, the page's background and translucent glass read substantially cooler and greyer than the screenshots.

The live hero features Fallout and its actual full-width key art. The supplied fixture catalogue has no Fallout asset, and the assignment forbids copying the user's actual title list. I staged the existing mock's Stranger Things with its local panoramic artwork, plus Dune: Part Two, Oppenheimer and The Batman as selectable alternatives. The descriptions, sequence of those four slides, their initial selected slide and the ghost letters are staged choices, not live application data.
The live screenshot's Next Up contents, artwork, season/episode labels, Continue Watching state and exact percentages depend on the private running library. The existing mock provides only six movies, three shows and three anime. I reused those twelve covers and their sample progress values; the displayed Next Up entries, "Up next" labels, Library membership and saved-for-later choices are therefore illustrative. Poster rails deliberately rotate and repeat those same twelve known titles rather than inventing hundreds of replacements. Their appearance should not be interpreted as accurate genre, rating, decade, service or shelf membership. Live catalogue poster content and poster density beyond the first twelve fixtures cannot be matched offline from the supplied assets.

The 19-theatre Discover capture includes specific live providers and source icons not all shipped as matching mock assets. I used the provided Torrentio, Netflix and TVDB images, plus locally drawn square C and T marks where the corresponding screenshot logos have no obvious matching fixture. I also staged a five-source sidebar order, a Cinemeta "Popular" heading, a simple working genre dropdown and 138×208px Discover cards. This set and its provider names are illustrative, not an assertion about the user's installed extensions. Real live catalogue sorting, filtering and pagination cannot run without the app's providers, so See all, details, playback, world switching and service selection are deliberately preview-only rather than pretending to connect.

The screenshots' live Shows index contains HBO Max, Disney+, Prime Video, Netflix and Apple TV+ as contextual sources; these are user-installed service rows and therefore absent from the house-only QML row inventory. TheatreCatalogRules.js defines service placement slots in a different effective order, namely Netflix, Prime, Disney, HBO/Max and Apple TV+. For the static Shows page I chose the visual order present in the live screenshot, while retaining the house shelf sequence. This is a deliberate screenshot-over-QML choice, not a claim that the user's installed extension state has been recovered.

The screenshot top bar has a sun/wallpaper glyph near the right edge, whereas TopBar.qml's corresponding pinned source describes a settings.svg system button; I used a small inline sun glyph to follow the capture. The original mock also used an account circle where the live screenshot instead shows a "Device" capsule, and it omitted the Stremio and tracker marks. I matched the screenshot's capsule and local Stremio/trackers icons, with staged status dots, rather than following the older mock. The clock/date display is read from the render-time machine clock like TopBar.qml's live clock, so it naturally differs from the minute in the references. The live screenshot's viewport is 1920×1008 physical pixels at 150% scaling; the explicit website contract is 1280×720 CSS pixels, which leaves a little more bottom content visible than a 1280×672 comparison image.

Other approximations are the hero's dark gradient/scrim intensity, the glass blur strength and shadow, icon/label source marks, collapsed index scroll offsets, availability of series-specific episode subtitles, preview toast wording, genre tile cover pairing and visible first-row ranking in every deep shelf. The underlying QML has asynchronous real data, saved row customization, per-tab preferences and viewport-lazy poster residency. This static fixture cannot reproduce those mechanisms faithfully and does not attempt to simulate network transport.
