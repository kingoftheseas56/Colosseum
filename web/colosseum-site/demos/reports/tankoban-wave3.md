# Tankoban world HTML recreation, wave 3

I replaced the Tankoban demo with one self-contained HTML document using inline CSS and JavaScript, no frameworks, no external scripts, and no network image URLs. The fixed outer stage is 1280 by 720 CSS pixels; its one inner page scrolls vertically by wheel and a thin visible scrollbar. The top bar and two-icon bottom-left taskbar stay fixed. All five top-bar world pills appear, with Tankoban active and no Vinyl pill. The world selector, source controls, local cover images, typography, status affordances and glass surfaces were rebuilt by consulting the seven live app screenshots and the master QML sources.

Discover shows the 330-pixel featured hero, the four-option tab bar, Manga and Comics browsing lenses, Jikan/GetComics source affordances, Popular catalogue context, filter control and a poster wall. The hero starts on Invincible and can switch between four staged features through its dots. The hero Read and Details controls and title cards give local preview feedback rather than attempting to open or control Colosseum.

Manga displays Your Collection, Top in Tankoban — Manga, and Explore by Genre — Manga, in exactly that QML order. Comics displays Your Collection, Top in Tankoban — Comics, then Recently Available, Complete Runs, Near Complete, Omnibuses, Deluxe Editions, Graphic Novels, Community Collections, Most Stocked, Marvel, DC, Image, The 2020s, The 2010s, Deep Shelves, Fan-Made Shelf, and Explore Comics. In production, only populated Comics shelves are rendered; this offline showcase stages them all so visitors can inspect the complete designed catalogue.

Both catalogue tabs use a row index on the left, collapsed to 52 pixels or expanded to 240 pixels. Its chevron opens and closes the labels, the selected shelf follows scrolling, and clicking a row parks it just below the compact tab bar. The SVG paths and their precedence follow RowIndexSidebar.qml's title-based rules exactly: collection/books, top/star, recent/clock, explore/compass, edition/book page, and fallback/list. This makes the unusual Complete Runs, Near Complete, and Omnibuses icons consistent with the actual QML rather than hand-selected icons.

Library shows a six-column cover wall with the QML search field, All/In Progress/Downloaded filters, Last Read/Recently Added/A-Z sort modes and per-card Remove from Library demo menu. These controls operate on the staged dataset only; they do not change the real collection. Switching among Discover, Manga, Comics and Library replaces just the page content, without navigation or a server.

Measurements directly taken from QML include the 54-pixel world margin, fixed TopBar at y=30, scroller starting at y=96, hero height 330, board gap 36, 640-by-54 in-flow tab glass, 520-by-46 compact docked tab glass, row-index widths 52/240 and left-content offset 28, index toggle size 28, genre tile height 112, genre gap 16, and taskbar size 130-by-64 with a 16-pixel bottom gap. The top-bar pills come from TopBar.qml, not the older first-generation mock with the extra Vinyl item.

The provided accepted feria.html established the nonresponsive stage and relative-asset approach. The earlier current-ui-mock world HTML, world-catalogue.js/CSS and its twelve manga/comics cover fixtures supplied the staged titles, images and original catalogue patterns. All recreated media resolves locally.

## Screenshot differences and unavoidable approximations

All seven screenshot files were opened before implementation. The running app screenshots use a dark sunset-city wallpaper, while the assignment explicitly requires ../assets/cold-ripple.jpg. That asset is the monochrome photograph of a pier in fog, so the overall hue, poster contrast, and appearance of frosted glass differ substantially. I followed the wallpaper path required by the assignment rather than copying screenshot pixels or introducing a replacement image. Qt's Glass.qml composites and blurs the actual scene through GPU effects; CSS backdrop-filter, scrims, gradients and borders only approximate that material.

The screenshots are 1920 by 1008 physical pixels, equivalent to an application canvas of 1280 by 672 CSS pixels at 150 percent scaling. The requested website contract instead has a fixed 1280-by-720 stage. The bottom of the scroll view and dock consequently occupy a different vertical position within the full image, although their offset rules follow the QML.

The screenshot's current featured item is Invincible, which the existing mock fixture contains, so that item could be reproduced with a local cover. Other live carousel slides were not shown and are represented by mock titles. The actual live collection, server catalogue rankings, number of saved entries, comic-series IDs, progress, download state, and available Comics shelves vary by profile and backend and cannot be recreated from a frozen screenshot without copying the user's personal list. I intentionally used the existing mock titles and cover files, rotating those same six comic covers where the real engine supplies larger and different shelves. Repeated art, fewer unique rankings, and catalogue popularity counts are therefore visibly nonidentical.

The Discover screenshot's dynamic source catalogue and its live cards could not be reproduced exactly without running the local backend. The offline version uses local Jikan and GetComics artwork and a static representative Popular wall. Genre-page and comic-series-page screenshots were visual references for the font, glass, cover treatment and spacing only, and their separate destinations were not embedded because this assignment is the one scrolling world page.

The screenshots do not display Next Up and Continue Reading between the hero and tabs. TankobanWorld.qml lines 196-240 nevertheless declare these two dynamic rows before WorldTabBar.qml. Their live models can be empty and the screenshot wins for the photographed state, so I omitted them from this static recreation rather than inventing rows. That is the only deliberate omission of a declaratively present world-level widget.

GenreMosaic.qml lines 41-54 calculate five flexible tile widths from the current content width, which would narrow the tiles after the row index expands. Screenshots 05 and 06 instead show nearly unchanged wide tile footprints, with the open index revealing fewer columns and the rightmost content cropped. I used a fixed 222-pixel tile width and 16-pixel gaps to match the photographed crop; this specifically departs from the formula in the current master QML. The screenshot takes precedence under the supplied instructions.

The QML's Comics catalogue initialization filters out shelves whose backend returns zero records, while the visual recreation intentionally keeps every shelf title in its declared order. The screenshots do not show each of those lower Comics shelves, so their staged cover sequences are illustrative, not asserted to be a live frame. The screenshot's user-specific Library wall likewise has a different set of covers from the permitted mock fixtures.

I rendered the fixed stage in headless Chrome with local files and inspected the results visually against the seven reference screenshots. The final scrolled render is reports/tankoban-wave3-scrolled.png, produced from tankoban.html?tab=manga&y=975&index=open and showing the compact dock, expanded index and Manga genre mosaic.

I additionally rendered the Library tab at y=520 and the Comics tab at y=930 during validation by temporarily reusing the two designated capture filenames; both showed the intended in-place content, and I replaced those temporary views with the required final top and Manga scrolled captures. I did not launch or interact with the running Colosseum application, edit any source checkout, use network imagery, or alter other site demos.
