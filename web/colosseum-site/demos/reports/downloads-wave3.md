# Downloads, wave 3: screenshot-led HTML recreation

The completed page is at `C:\Users\Suprabha\Desktop\Brotherhood\agents\colosseum-site\demos\downloads.html`. I replaced the older Downloads demo, which was a script-driven manager with “All worlds” filters and ledger buttons rather than the real screen's editorial header, Now arriving area and cover shelves. The replacement is one 1280×720 fixed HTML stage with its own vertically scrolling inner viewport. Its only dependencies are local image, icon and Fraunces font paths. Its CSS and JavaScript are inline, with no framework, remote URL or external script.

The page shows the “COLOSSEUM · LOCAL” eyebrow, Fraunces Downloads heading, short gold rule, a single inline totals sentence, one live Now arriving job with progress and Pause/Cancel actions, and the Tankoban, Biblio, Theatre and Audiobooks sections in that order. The taskbar remains stationary while the content scrolls; the home control expands the capsule from the default two permanent icons to the seven shown in screenshot 15. Back/search/window controls remain fixed. A discreet chevron also opens a page-section index, hidden at rest because neither screenshot shows a visible index.

## Deliberate departures and things an HTML still cannot duplicate

The two screenshots show different live app wallpapers: one is an abstract dark ribbon scene and the other shows a night city. The stated page contract instead explicitly requires `../assets/cold-ripple.jpg`, so I used that photograph behind the dark QML-style wash. No screenshot pixels, crops or user-library art were copied. The backdrop and translucent panels cannot reproduce Qt's exact shader, texture sampling, lighting or desktop glass; these are CSS approximations. The real screenshot's footer may therefore feel more photographic and less uniform.

Screenshot 15 contains a failed Theatre job with a Retry action. Screenshot 14 has a Now arriving heading reporting zero live jobs and one attention item, but no visible job panel. The prompt explicitly calls for one **in-progress** item and no failed download, so the demo instead shows an active Arcane episode with a thin gold progress indicator and Pause/Cancel. The QML normally hides the Now arriving column when it has no jobs; this staged state has one.

The actual app screenshots have **no world pills** on Downloads, and `DownloadsPage.qml` paints its own back/search/minimize/fullscreen/power controls rather than the ordinary world picker. For that reason I omitted the five world pills here, despite the general Feria page contract's instruction for pages that visibly have a picker. Downloads is an app-wide taskbar destination, never a tab of a selected world. There is also no Vinyl pill.

The screenshot shows completed covers from Hemanth's actual library, including occasional blank gradient fallbacks; I did not replicate those entries. Instead I used the covers and names under the existing mock assets. The mock has only two suitable book-cover files, so Biblio and Audiobooks have two cards each rather than invented images. The screenshot's cover-image availability, ordering and resulting color balance cannot be exactly matched without using his personal library or obtaining new assets, which was outside scope.

The Audiobooks section is a deliberate user-contract override: `DownloadsPage.qml` renders completed audiobook entries in compact 54px rows rather than artwork cards, but this assignment explicitly requests a glass cover-card panel **for each section**, including Audiobooks. I followed the assignment. The optional available-on-another-device lane and QML ledger controls were not staged because the requested sequence is Now arriving followed by four local cover shelves, with no remote-device fixture.

The photographs are 1920×1008 at 150% Windows scaling, corresponding to a 1280×672 CSS-pixel app view. The required website stage is 1280×720. Thus the fixed taskbar sits 48 CSS pixels lower in the website capture than its position measured relative to the source screenshot's upper edge, even though it uses the QML bottom gap of 16px. The attached screenshots document this unavoidable canvas-height difference. The optional section index does not appear in either reference screenshot, so its handle is transparent until hover/focus; it can be opened by clicking the handle, pressing I or supplying `?index=open`. There are no native world tabs on Downloads: `?tab=tankoban`, `?tab=biblio`, `?tab=theatre` and `?tab=audiobooks` jump to the named page section instead of creating a fake tab strip.

## Invented staging values

The **entire** number and disk accounting is invented, not copied from the screenshots: 52 stored items and 33.4 GB total, divided into Tankoban 42 items/3.2 GB, Biblio 2 books/20 MB, Theatre 6 files/28.4 GB, and Audiobooks 2/1.8 GB. These approximate sizes add to 33.42 GB, shown rounded to 33.4 GB. The seven Tankoban mock cards say 12, 7, 6, 4, 5, 6 and 2 volumes/issues, respectively. Biblio's two books, Theatre's six films and Audiobooks' two titles each have an invented single-file or single-book count.

The active mock job is “Arcane · Season 2, Episode 3” in Theatre, 68% complete, with 1.8 GB of 2.6 GB, 2.8 MB/s and approximately five minutes remaining. Its title, item identity, progress, transfer quantities, ETA, ownership and action state are entirely fixture values. No actual download is started, no storage totals are queried, and the expanded card detail rows are illustrative. Opening a card does not launch a real player. Pause/Resume changes the displayed state locally, and Cancel removes the staged job from the page. Non-Downloads taskbar surfaces can link to the corresponding local sibling demo or report that a desktop-only action is unavailable. The mock does not delete real files or sign in.
