# Code of Conduct

Colosseum's project spaces are for building, testing, documenting, and discussing the software. Participation should make those spaces useful to people with different backgrounds and levels of experience.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful and specific when discussing code, design, defects, and decisions.
- Critique ideas and changes rather than attacking people.
- Give others room to ask basic questions, disagree, and correct mistakes.
- Keep feedback relevant to the project and the conversation at hand.
- Respect privacy. Do not publish another person's personal information, private messages, credentials, or identifying data without permission.
- Accept maintainer decisions about scope and moderation without harassment or repeated disruption.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, threats, discriminatory or demeaning language, sexualized attention, stalking, doxxing, sustained personal attacks, deliberate intimidation, and other conduct that makes participation unsafe or hostile are not acceptable.

Spam, coordinated disruption, impersonation, and knowingly posting malicious or sensitive material are also outside the project boundary.

## Scope

This code applies to Colosseum repository spaces and to project-related interactions when someone is representing the project or its maintainers.

## Reporting conduct problems

For platform-level abuse, use GitHub's own reporting tools. When a Colosseum maintainer needs to act, use a private contact route exposed by the maintainer where possible.

If no private contact route is available, open only a minimal issue asking for private contact. Do not publish sensitive personal details or evidence in the issue body.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, close or lock threads, reject contributions, or restrict participation when behavior violates this code. Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and focused on protecting a constructive project space.

Good-faith technical disagreement is not a conduct violation. The standard is how people are treated while disagreeing.
